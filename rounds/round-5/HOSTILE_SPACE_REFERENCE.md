# Hostile Space Reference Lineage

Hostile Space is the historical design reference for the hostile-space experiment. The current fan revival describes the original 1997 game as a top-down, real-time 2D science-fiction MMORPG set in a persistent galaxy, with exploration, ship upgrades, cooperative missions, mining, trading, and bounty hunting. See the [Hostile Space Revived project site](https://hostilespace.co.uk/).

This is lineage and behavioral inspiration, not a cloning target. `STARBLAST_REFERENCE.md` owns the 3D presentation reference; the approved V2 mockups remain the visual oracle for Round 5A.

## Preserve as design invariants

- The sector is a navigable place, not an abstract background: landmarks, contacts, wrecks, pickups, and terrain or debris communicate location and motion.
- Ship roles read quickly from silhouette, color family, scale, and relative motion.
- The player remains readable while a station or other landmark establishes scale.
- World and server events create the sense of a persistent inhabited galaxy.
- Cargo, scan, target, hull, and radar information support decisions without replacing the world view.
- Combat and interaction stay legible at gameplay distance rather than depending on cinematic close-ups.

## Modernize deliberately

- Keep the playfield open instead of reproducing a permanent 4:3 bottom control slab.
- Use contextual and automatic actions instead of copying dense rows of small buttons or a permanent fire control.
- Collapse cargo, scan, and status detail into compact edge HUD or contextual panels.
- Use responsive desktop and portrait layouts with touch-safe controls.
- Preserve event meaning with a short readable feed rather than a large terminal transcript over the playfield.
- Use procedural low-poly 3D depth, lighting, and world-anchored motion while preserving top-down tactical readability.

## Exclude from implementation

- Exact ship, station, icon, terrain, panel, typeface, logo, name, or trade-dress reproduction.
- Screenshot pixels as runtime textures, tracing sources, or generated-image inputs.
- Literal copying of labels, messages, character names, or UI arrangement.
- Bright green planetary terrain as a universal space background.
- Pixel-era clutter or low resolution treated as a quality target.
